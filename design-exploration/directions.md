# Ordalin — four design theses

Status: completed. **04 · Quiet Radar** is the approved main theme. The four theses below are retained as decision evidence; they are not four active implementation options.

## 01 · Editorial Index

**Thesis:** Make the catalogue feel like a contemporary reference publication whose judgment is visible in its pacing and labels.

- Feels like: composed, literate, durable, selectively opinionated.
- Reference mix: Monocle for editorial hierarchy + Are.na for restraint + Product Hunt for readable daily inventory.
- Signature move: a numbered vertical register links Categories, Today, Picks, and Collections as sections of one authored index.
- Composition: three-column editorial grid; category register left, inventory column central, notes and collections right.
- Hierarchy: content-first; search is concise and inventory begins immediately.
- Typography: Georgia display and section labels paired with neutral system sans for metadata.
- Color/material: warm paper, charcoal ink, oxblood editorial accent, fine rules; no cards floating over cards.
- Shape/imagery: rectangular fields, almost no radius, letter-mark tool icons, no photography.
- Motion: short underline and rule transitions; rows reveal metadata without displacement.
- Light theme: warm off-white surface with dark ink; no dark theme in MVP.
- Strongest advantage: best alignment with the approved Editorial Cut identity and trust goal.
- Main risk: may feel serious to users expecting a playful AI marketplace.
- Avoid when: the brand later prioritizes entertainment, social voting, or creator-style promotion.
- Refuses: magazine imitation, large lifestyle photography, ornate serif styling.

## 02 · Signal Desk

**Thesis:** Present discovery as a precise instrument where every control and status earns its place.

- Feels like: fast, exact, transparent, operational.
- Reference mix: Linear for grid discipline + AlternativeTo for visible filters + G2 for controlled taxonomy.
- Signature move: a compact signal strip summarizes catalogue state before a table-like feed.
- Composition: strict 12-column workbench with fixed left filter rail, dense central results, compact right intelligence panel.
- Hierarchy: action-first and state-first; search, date, filter, result count, and provenance are continuously legible.
- Typography: technical grotesk with monospaced labels and tabular numerals.
- Color/material: cool white and graphite with safety orange and cobalt used only for active/status roles.
- Shape/imagery: tight geometry, 2–6 px radii, structural panels, glyph-like letter marks.
- Motion: direct 120 ms state changes and row highlight; no atmospheric movement.
- Light theme: cool neutral hierarchy with strong focus outlines.
- Strongest advantage: fastest scanning and clearest deterministic behavior.
- Main risk: can intimidate casual users or feel too much like an internal database.
- Avoid when: broad approachability matters more than expert efficiency.
- Refuses: soft cards, decorative gradients, speculative metrics, hidden filters.

## 03 · Open Atlas

**Thesis:** Turn a large catalogue into an approachable map of distinct territories without making it childish.

- Feels like: welcoming, exploratory, memorable, generous.
- Reference mix: Raindrop.io for collection entry points + Futurepedia for familiar category labels + AlternativeTo for browseability.
- Signature move: category “territories” use restrained color tabs that continue into active filter and card accents.
- Composition: broad horizontal category rail above a two-column inventory mosaic and editorial sidebar.
- Hierarchy: context-first; users choose a territory, then scan tools within it.
- Typography: humanist system sans with a soft serif only for the promise.
- Color/material: parchment base with clay, moss, ochre, and ink-blue semantic category accents.
- Shape/imagery: modular blocks, medium radius, stamped letter marks, no generated imagery.
- Motion: category selection reorders color emphasis with a short spatial slide; reduced motion uses instant color/state change.
- Light theme: warm neutral ground; colored regions retain accessible dark text.
- Strongest advantage: categories become memorable and approachable for newcomers.
- Main risk: too much color could weaken Ordalin’s editorial authority as inventory grows.
- Avoid when: the catalogue needs very high row density above all else.
- Refuses: rainbow decoration, mascot illustration, playful bounce, oversized feature tiles.

## 04 · Quiet Radar

**Thesis:** Make discovery feel like calm orientation: a broad search field, low-noise spatial cues, and quietly surfaced editorial signals.

- Feels like: perceptive, calm, premium, lightly futuristic without AI clichés.
- Reference mix: Cosmos for provenance and search-as-orientation + Are.na for intentional discovery + Linear for restrained product polish.
- Signature move: a subtle coordinate field connects the active category, feed state, and editorial notes without literal radar graphics.
- Composition: centered compact promise and search above a layered three-zone field; categories are a quiet horizontal index.
- Hierarchy: search-first but not search-only; results remain visible in the first viewport.
- Typography: contemporary sans with wide display spacing and monospaced coordinates for metadata.
- Color/material: mist white, pale mineral blue, graphite, single luminous green for verified freshness.
- Shape/imagery: broad translucent-looking fields implemented as solid performant layers, 14–24 px radii, no photography.
- Motion: 180 ms cross-fade and anchored panel shift; reduced motion keeps only opacity/state feedback.
- Light theme: low-chroma surfaces with strong dark text; transparency has an opaque fallback.
- Strongest advantage: a distinctive “navigation radar” feeling without using a literal radar or AI sparkle.
- Main risk: low-contrast atmosphere can weaken boundaries if implementation drifts from the specified tokens.
- Avoid when: the product must feel aggressively dense or publication-like.
- Refuses: glowing orbs, starfields, glass blur over moving backgrounds, literal radar sweeps.
