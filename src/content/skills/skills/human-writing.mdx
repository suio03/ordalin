## What we tested

We wrote a short English post about three weeks with the Pomodoro Technique in the style people call “AI-sounding”: a sweeping opening line, a Firstly/Secondly/Finally skeleton, an “isn't just X, it's Y” turn, two dashes, “game-changer” and an uplifting last line. We asked Claude Code to rewrite it as a personal blog post, keeping only the facts already in the draft. One run loaded the skill; the other used the same model and the same request without it. We also ran the skill's own `check_prose.py` on all three texts.

## What we found

- **Both versions kept every number.** Four sessions a day in week one, seven after the phone left the room, 50-minute sessions for the weekly report.
- **The Chinese rules carried over to English.** The skill version had no colons, no dashes and no headings, and its sentences ran from short to long. The version without the skill turned the post into four bold sections and used four colons.
- **Both added things the draft does not say, the version without the skill more so.** With the skill, the model claimed the phone was “the only change I made” and that the writer still uses the timer. Without it, the model invented small scenes, such as the timer running while focus did not, and the 25 minutes ending “just as I got going”.
- **The checker does not work in English.** On all three texts it stopped with a message that it found no Chinese characters, so in English you check the result by reading it.

On a strong model the gain was real but moderate: the draft's clichés were gone in both versions, and the skill mainly added a plainer shape and slightly better restraint. One short rewrite is a small test. We have not yet tried long articles or weaker models, where the skill's material check and revision passes would have more to do.
