## What we tested

We wrote a short English post about a first year running a plant stall at a Saturday market, in the style people call “AI-sounding”: a “Let's be honest” opening, a “Here's the thing” run-up, an “isn't just X, it's about Y” turn, three bold labels, a list of three, two dashes, “a true testament”, “At its core” and two emoji. It holds a dozen small facts we could check, from 40 cuttings and 12 sales on the first day to the $1,800 donation.

We pasted it into Claude Code with the same one-line request twice. One run had the skill installed in the project; the other used the same model with nothing installed. Both runs loaded project settings only, so no personal instructions or installed skills were involved beyond Claude Code's built-in ones and, in one run, Humanizer.

## What we found

- **Both versions kept every fact.** 40 cuttings and 12 sold, $8 down to $5, nearly double the sales, the pet-safe labels, every week in all weather, about 60 plants by December and the $1,800 donation.
- **Only the version without the skill made things up.** It added a deflated drive home, an evening spent wondering about prices, regulars who come back to show how their cuttings are doing and the claim that “the plants pay for themselves”. It warned about the regulars line but not the others. The skill version added no facts. It did add one reaction, that the donation is the part the writers are proudest of, and its own check flagged that line before the final version.
- **The skill showed its work.** Its reply had a first draft, a list of four patterns still in that draft (a vague “trust and loyalty” pair, a short closer, a softened “not X but Y” contrast and the added reaction) and the final version. The run without the skill returned one rewrite and a list of changes.
- **Both removed the obvious tells.** Neither kept the dashes, emoji, “Here's the thing” or “a true testament”. Neither kept a colon. The version without the skill kept a bold title, and the skill version came out shorter, 159 words against 209.
- **Some stock lines survived.** The skill version kept “We can't wait to see what year two brings” as its last line and turned “a valuable lesson in patience” into “meant we had to be patient”.

The skill run took three turns instead of one and cost about three times as much at API prices ($0.20 against $0.07, as reported by Claude Code), mostly because it writes a draft and a check before the final version. One short post on a strong model is a small test. We have not tried long articles, files, voice matching or weaker models. For Chinese text, see our test of [Human Writing](/skills/human-writing).
