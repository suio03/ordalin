## What we tested

We gave Claude Code two videos whose making we know, so we could check its answers.

- **A 15-second Kling 3.0 clip**: a woman in a cream coat in a dark hallway, with a white mask, in four shots, generated with Kling 3.0 at 854 × 480 by Ordalin's maintainer.
- **A 78-second vertical explainer made by Ordalin's maintainer**: motion graphics rendered in code with Remotion, real stock photos, one filmed stock clip of hands holding a phone with a chat screen composited on, captions, a text-to-speech voice, library music and sound effects.

For each one we ran the same request in a fresh folder: install the tool from GitHub into a virtual environment, break the video down with the rea-video skill, do the visual review yourself because no vision model is configured, and answer in English. The skill was loaded from the repository at commit faadcfd (version 0.1.0). Both runs installed the tool, ran decompose, review, apply-review and summary, pulled extra frame sheets for close calls, and finished without help.

## What we found

- **The AI clip was called right, for the right reasons.** All four Kling shots ended as AI video. The tool alone had called the hallway walk 3D; the agent changed it to AI video, citing wool, hair and leather that looked real and doors that shifted as she walked. Its evidence was concrete: a bag strap that appears from nowhere, a hand on the door frame that turns to mush.
- **It said what it could not know.** It put the chance of a filmed 24p shoot at about 15%, guessed a Kling-style multi-shot model only from the even shot lengths, and left the music's source unconfirmed because it could not listen.
- **The report got our explainer wrong, and stayed wrong.** The tool read the file's Remotion tag but still labelled the video 47% AI video, 44% 3D and 9% code-rendered. The agent reviewed all 20 shots and said it was nearly all code-rendered motion graphics, which is right. The merge then changed only one shot, so report.html still carries the wrong split and a remake plan of 14 AI video generations. The agent's reply flagged the disagreement, wrote its own corrected table to a separate file and suggested adding the video to the tool's test set.
- **The filmed clip stayed unconfirmed.** The agent could not tell whether the hands-and-phone shot was stock footage or AI video and left it at roughly even odds; the report calls it AI video. It did see that the chat screen was added afterwards. It also called the voice likely text-to-speech, which was right.
- **The edit and captions came out in detail.** It found 42 caption lines with their style and timing, and 35 sound-effect candidates. It warned that its count of 433 text labels was inflated by the numbers inside the animation.
- **Everything except the reply is in Chinese.** Both reports and every generated prompt were in Chinese, though we asked for English and the agent answered in English.

The runs took 24 and 30 turns, 3 and 5 minutes, and $0.57 and $0.84 at API prices, as reported by Claude Code. These are two videos with one model, so read them as examples, not a benchmark.

## The author's own figures

The README reports how often the shot type is right, weighted by shot duration, on its own test videos:

- **Original files, tools only (offline, free):** 98.3% on the tuning set of 15 videos, 99.6% and 98.9% on two held-out sets of 9 and 7.
- **Recompressed downloads, 10 videos:** 88.6% with tools only, 99.6% with a Gemini vision model, 100% with the agent's visual review.

It also reports 97 to 99% for finding real embedded material and every shot boundary correct. The README notes that platform recompression wipes out the noise evidence the tools rely on, which is why downloaded videos need a visual check.
