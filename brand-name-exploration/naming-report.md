# Brand name exploration

Research date: 22 August 2026
Product source: `MVP_PLAN.md` — approved “Navigation Radar” concept
Market: global English, United States primary and Australia secondary
Product route: web-first AI tool directory; no iOS or Android name screening requested

## Final decision

**Ordalin** is the selected brand and repository name. The remaining names below are retained only as research history, not as active candidates.

## Original recommendation

The original shortlist was:

1. **Ordalin** — the strongest link to ordering, indexing, and navigating a large catalogue without sounding descriptive.
2. **Curnio** — the most compact and ownable option; all three requested domains were registrar-reported available.
3. **Lorinva** — the most brand-like and expandable option; softer and more editorial than the surrounding AI-directory category.

This shortlist preceded the final selection of Ordalin. The research remains a lightweight collision screen, not formal legal clearance. Domain status can change at any time.

## Naming brief

The product is an inventory-first directory for discovering reviewed AI tools through categories, search, filters, new arrivals, editor picks, and collections. The name therefore needs to feel trustworthy, clear, perceptive, and editorial—not like a chatbot, an autonomous agent, or a growth-hacker product.

Hard constraints used:

- English letters only
- 5–9 letters and 2–3 spoken syllables
- Brandable rather than keyword-stuffed
- At least one registrar-reported available domain among `.com`, `.io`, and `.app`
- Avoid crowded constructions such as `AI + noun`, `Tool + suffix`, `Future + noun`, `Top + AI`, `Hub`, and `Hunt`

## Competitive collision map

Direct competitors heavily occupy explicit category language: [Futurepedia](https://www.futurepedia.io/), [Future Tools](https://futuretools.io/), [Toolify](https://www.toolify.ai/), [TopAI.tools](https://topai.tools/), and [AIxploria](https://www.aixploria.com/en/). Adjacent discovery brands occupy action and container metaphors: [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/), [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/), [SaaSHub](https://www.saashub.com/), and [Uneed](https://www.uneed.best/).

The opportunity is a standalone coined word: easy to say, calm enough to trust, and broad enough to grow beyond a list of AI tools.

## Domain evidence

Availability was checked in [Namecheap Beast Mode](https://www.namecheap.com/domains/registration/results/?type=beast&domain=) on 22 August 2026 in AUD. “Registrar reports available” means the registrar showed a current price and an **Add to cart** action. “Registered” means the registrar showed the domain as taken/registered or offered an owner-contact path. No domain was purchased or reserved.

Registrar output is the availability authority used here. Search-engine absence, DNS failure, or an empty registration lookup was not treated as proof of availability.

## Trademark evidence and limits

Each of the 12 exact words was checked in the official [USPTO Trademark Search](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/search/) and [Australian Trade Mark Search](https://search.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademarks). Both systems returned 0 exact results for every candidate at the time checked.

This is a lightweight knockout screen only. The official guidance emphasizes searching confusingly similar marks and related goods/services, not only exact words: [USPTO federal trademark searching](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching) and [IP Australia search guidance](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/trade-marks/search-existing-trade-marks). A trademark professional should clear the selected name before launch or filing.

For the deeper finalist screen, the inferred Nice classes are:

- **Class 42** — web software/SaaS and search technology
- **Class 35** — online directory, business information, advertising, and marketplace-adjacent services

Please confirm those classes before the next clearance pass. Class 9 may also become relevant if downloadable software is added later.

## Decision guide

- Choose **Ordalin** if the product should feel most connected to organised navigation and a trustworthy index.
- Choose **Curnio** if compactness, memorability, and full domain flexibility matter most.
- Choose **Lorinva** if the long-term ambition is a broader editorial discovery brand rather than only an AI tool directory.

Recommended next test: show only these three names, without explanations, to 5–8 native or fluent English speakers. Ask each person to pronounce the name, type it after hearing it once, and describe what kind of company they expect it to be. Do not test preference alone.
